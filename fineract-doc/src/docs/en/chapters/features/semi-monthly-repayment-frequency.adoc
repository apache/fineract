= Semi-Monthly Repayment Frequency

== Purpose

The semi-monthly (half-monthly) repayment frequency repays a loan twice every calendar month on two fixed days, for
example the 1st and the 15th, or the 15th and the last day of the month. It targets payroll loans, salary advances and
consumer loans whose repayments have to follow the borrower's pay cycle.

None of the existing frequencies produces this schedule:

* a 15-day interval drifts through the month over time;
* a bi-weekly schedule (every 2 weeks) yields 26 repayments a year instead of 24;
* a monthly schedule has only one due date a month.

A semi-monthly schedule always yields exactly two due dates per calendar month, which is 24 repayments a year.

== Configuration

Semi-monthly is a new value of `repaymentFrequencyType`:

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
| Value | Code | Description

| `6`
| `periodFrequencyType.semiMonthly`
| Two repayments every calendar month on two fixed days.
|===

The value `6` is placed after `INVALID(5)` on purpose. Frequencies are persisted by value, so inserting it before
`INVALID` would change the meaning of rows that already exist.

Both due days are configured:

[cols="2,2,3"]
|===
| Field | Allowed values | Notes

| `firstRepaymentDayOfMonth`
| 1 to 27
| The first due day of every month.

| `secondRepaymentDayOfMonth`
| 2 to 31, greater than `firstRepaymentDayOfMonth`
| The second due day. A day the month does not have falls on its last day, so `31` is the last day of every month.
|===

The two days do not have to be evenly spread: pay cycles are not always 14 or 15 days apart, so a pair such as the 1st
and the 25th is valid. There is no minimum gap between them.

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
| First due day | Second due day | Due dates

| `1`
| `15`
| 1st and 15th

| `15`
| `31`
| 15th and 28th/29th February, 30th April, 31st July

| `1`
| `25`
| 1st and 25th

| `5`
| `30`
| 5th and 30th; 28th/29th in February
|===

=== Why the first day stops at 27

The second due day is capped at the length of the month. With a first due day of 28, every valid second day (29, 30
or 31) falls on the 28th in a non-leap February, so both repayments of that month would land on the same date. Capping
the first day at 27 keeps the two due dates distinct in every month and every year.

== Rules

=== Product

* `firstRepaymentDayOfMonth` and `secondRepaymentDayOfMonth` are both required when `repaymentFrequencyType` is `6`.
* The first day must be between 1 and 27, the second between 2 and 31, and the second must be greater than the first.
* The two days are set together: a create or update request that sends only one of them is rejected.
* Both days are rejected on any other frequency.
* `repaymentEvery` must be `1`: two repayments a month is the frequency itself, it cannot repeat every two periods.
* Semi-monthly is available with both loan schedule types, `CUMULATIVE` and `PROGRESSIVE`.

=== Loan

* A loan may override the product's days by sending its own `firstRepaymentDayOfMonth` and
`secondRepaymentDayOfMonth`, for example to follow a specific borrower's pay cycle. The days are overridden as a pair,
with the same ranges and ordering as on the product: one day of the loan next to the other one of the product could
break their order.
* A loan that becomes semi-monthly must end up with both days: either in the request, already stored on the loan, or on
the product. Otherwise the request is rejected; the schedule never falls back to default days silently.
* A semi-monthly loan cannot be synchronised with a group or center meeting (`calendarId`): meeting calendars have no
semi-monthly recurrence. A group or JLG loan that needs this frequency is handled like an individual loan.
* `loanTermFrequencyType` must be equal to `repaymentFrequencyType`, as for every other frequency. A term of six months
with twelve installments is therefore entered as a term of 12 semi-monthly periods.

==== Which days a loan uses

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Operation | Precedence

| Create
| The days in the request; otherwise the product's days.

| Modify
| The days in the request; otherwise the days the loan already has; otherwise, only when the loan has none (for
example it was monthly until now), the product's days.
|===

The two days always come from the same source. A modification that changes the days regenerates the schedule.

== Schedule generation

=== First due date

When no first repayment date is supplied, the first due date is the first configured due day strictly after the seed
date (the disbursement date or the submitted-on date, depending on the product's repayment start date type).

* Disbursed on the 12th with days 1 and 15: first due date is the 15th of the same month.
* Disbursed on the 20th with days 1 and 15: first due date is the 1st of the next month.

When the product defines `minimumDaysBetweenDisbursalAndFirstRepayment` and that gap ends after the date above, the first
due date moves to the first configured due day on or after the end of the gap, never to an arbitrary day. For example,
disbursed on 10 January with days 15 and last day and a gap of 10 days: the gap ends on 20 January, so the first due
date is 31 January.

When the caller supplies `repaymentsStartingFromDate`, it is used as it is, but it has to be one of the configured due
days. Any other date is rejected with
`error.msg.loan.application.first.repayment.date.not.a.semi.monthly.due.day`.

=== Following due dates

Each following due date is the next configured due day after the previous one. A second day of 31 resolves per month:
28th or 29th of February, 30th of April, 31st of July.

.Days 15 and last day, disbursed 5 January 2023
[cols="1,1"]
|===
| Installment | Due date

| 1 | 15 January 2023
| 2 | 31 January 2023
| 3 | 15 February 2023
| 4 | 28 February 2023
| 5 | 15 March 2023
| 6 | 31 March 2023
| 7 | 15 April 2023
| 8 | 30 April 2023
|===

.Days 1 and 25, disbursed 5 January 2023
[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Installment | Due date | Period length

| 1 | 25 January 2023 | 20 days (irregular first period)
| 2 | 1 February 2023 | 7 days
| 3 | 25 February 2023 | 24 days
| 4 | 1 March 2023 | 4 days
| 5 | 25 March 2023 | 24 days
| 6 | 1 April 2023 | 7 days
|===

=== Interest

The two periods of a month have different lengths, slightly (16 days from the 15th to the 31st of January, 13 days
from the 15th to the 28th of February) or a lot (24 and 7 days with the 1st and the 25th). Whether that difference shows in the interest depends on how the product
calculates it:

[cols="2,2,3"]
|===
| Schedule type | Product setting | Interest per semi-monthly period

| Cumulative
| Interest calculation period *same as repayment period*
| 1/24 of the annual rate, whatever the length of the period.

| Cumulative
| Interest calculation period *daily*
| The actual days of the period, at the daily rate (annual rate / days in year).

| Progressive
| Interest calculation period *same as repayment period*
| 1/24 of the annual rate, whatever the length of the period.

| Progressive
| Days in month *actual*
| The actual days of the period, at the daily rate (annual rate / days in year).

| Progressive
| Days in month *30*
| Half of a 30-day month, i.e. 15 days, whatever the actual length of the period.
|===

NOTE: Only the settings that work with actual days charge a short and a long period differently. With the other
settings every regular period costs the same, so with an uneven pair such as the 1st and the 25th a 7-day period costs
as much interest as a 24-day one. Products that use uneven pairs and must charge the actual days should use daily
interest calculation (cumulative) or actual days in month (progressive).

An irregular period, such as a first period that is shorter than a regular one because the loan was disbursed close to
the first due date, is prorated when the product calculates partial periods (daily interest, partial period interest
calculation, or 30-day months on progressive): each semi-monthly period the interval touches contributes the share of
its own days that the interval covers.

24 periods per year are used for the annual percentage rate and the periods-per-year calculations.

== API

=== Loan product

`POST /loanproducts` and `PUT /loanproducts/{productId}`

[source,json]
----
{
  "repaymentFrequencyType": 6,
  "repaymentEvery": 1,
  "firstRepaymentDayOfMonth": 1,
  "secondRepaymentDayOfMonth": 25
}
----

=== Loan

`POST /loans` and `PUT /loans/{loanId}`

[source,json]
----
{
  "loanTermFrequencyType": 6,
  "repaymentFrequencyType": 6,
  "repaymentEvery": 1,
  "firstRepaymentDayOfMonth": 10,
  "secondRepaymentDayOfMonth": 31
}
----

The days are optional on the loan, but they are sent together; see <<Which days a loan uses>>.
`GET /loanproducts/{productId}`, `GET /loans/template` and `GET /loans/{loanId}` return both configured days.

=== Validation errors

Every `validation.msg` code exists for the product (`validation.msg.loanproduct.…`) and for the loan
(`validation.msg.loan.…`).

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Code | Cause

| `validation.msg.loanproduct.firstRepaymentDayOfMonth.cannot.be.blank` +
`validation.msg.loanproduct.secondRepaymentDayOfMonth.cannot.be.blank`
| Semi-monthly product without both due days, or a request that sends only one of them.

| `validation.msg.loan.firstRepaymentDayOfMonth.cannot.be.blank` +
`validation.msg.loan.secondRepaymentDayOfMonth.cannot.be.blank`
| Semi-monthly loan with no pair in the request, on the loan or on the product, or a request that sends only one day.

| `validation.msg.loanproduct.firstRepaymentDayOfMonth.is.not.within.expected.range` +
`validation.msg.loan.firstRepaymentDayOfMonth.is.not.within.expected.range`
| First day outside 1 to 27.

| `validation.msg.loanproduct.secondRepaymentDayOfMonth.is.not.within.expected.range` +
`validation.msg.loan.secondRepaymentDayOfMonth.is.not.within.expected.range`
| Second day outside 2 to 31.

| `validation.msg.loanproduct.secondRepaymentDayOfMonth.must.be.greater.than.first.repayment.day.of.month` +
`validation.msg.loan.secondRepaymentDayOfMonth.must.be.greater.than.first.repayment.day.of.month`
| Second day equal to or before the first one.

| `validation.msg.loanproduct.firstRepaymentDayOfMonth.supported.only.for.semi.monthly.repayment.frequency` +
`validation.msg.loan.firstRepaymentDayOfMonth.supported.only.for.semi.monthly.repayment.frequency`
| Days sent with a frequency other than semi-monthly (reported on `secondRepaymentDayOfMonth` when only that one is
sent).

| `validation.msg.loanproduct.repaymentEvery.must.be.one.for.semi.monthly.repayment.frequency` +
`validation.msg.loan.repaymentEvery.must.be.one.for.semi.monthly.repayment.frequency`
| `repaymentEvery` other than 1.

| `validation.msg.loan.calendarId.not.supported.for.semi.monthly.repayment.frequency`
| Semi-monthly loan synchronised with a meeting calendar.

| `error.msg.loan.application.first.repayment.date.not.a.semi.monthly.due.day`
| `repaymentsStartingFromDate` is not one of the configured due days.

| `error.msg.loan.semi.monthly.repayment.days.missing`
| A schedule is generated for a semi-monthly loan that lacks one of its due days. The validators prevent this, so it
signals inconsistent data rather than a user error.
|===

== Limitations

* Loans synchronised with a group or center meeting calendar are not supported (see <<Loan>>).
* Semi-monthly is a loan repayment frequency only. It is not offered for savings interest rate charts or standing
instructions.
